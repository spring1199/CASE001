# Project Vision

## Elevator pitch
The player receives a missing man's phone marked only with the number **18473**. By reading messages, photos, system logs, receipts, calls and erased data, she attempts to find him. The investigation gradually reveals that the missing man knew her, protected her, and that she herself participated in the city-scale system at the center of the case—before an accident erased the relevant autobiographical memories.

## Genre
Psychological detective thriller; digital investigation; grounded techno-political drama; tragic romance beneath the mystery.

## Emotional target
The player should move through:
1. curiosity,
2. familiarity,
3. intimacy,
4. attraction,
5. suspicion,
6. rejection,
7. recognition,
8. guilt,
9. hope,
10. impossible choice,
11. grief.

The desired ending response is: first stunned silence, then sadness strong enough to make the player cry.

## Moral thesis
> A right choice may exist, but a right choice is not free.

Secondary question:
> If you protect someone by taking away their ability to choose, is that love or control?

## Story law
No important character is purely correct or purely wrong. Every consequential action should save a value and sacrifice another.

## Fair-play law
The player can, in principle, solve every major reveal early. The game hides meaning, not facts.

## Tone
- contemporary and realistic;
- restrained rather than melodramatic;
- tense rather than horror-driven;
- mature themes are allowed;
- no supernatural explanation;
- no magical hacking;
- no omnipotent AI.

## Setting: Arvantai
Arvantai is a fictional present-day Mongolian capital: dense apartment districts, old blocks, modern compounds, SÖH building management, convenience stores, QR payments, parking gates, traffic, ministry offices, cold winters, peripheral roads, ger-district-adjacent zones and nearby countryside.

All brands/services should be fictional but instantly understandable.
